## The one-liner card

**中文：**

> **WhiteHartLane — 别搭流程，让它自己长。**
> 交给它一个文件夹：一队 AI 帮手各坐各的窗口、各看各的文件、靠传话协作，
> 无人值守从头跑到尾；全程留记录——今天的运行，就是明天更省的流程。

**English:**

> **WhiteHartLane — Don't build workflows. Grow them.**
> Hand it a folder: a crew of AI helpers, each in its own window, sealed to
> its own files, coordinating by messages — unattended, start to finish.
> Every step recorded; today's run becomes tomorrow's leaner workflow.

> run becomes tomorrow's lean workflow. Loop engineers and RSI tinkerers

## Market trend

**中文：**
单聊不够用了，大家开始养一支帮手队伍。现有两条路：把流程写成代码（太重），
或让模型自己当管家（账不可信）。第三条路：管事的是程序，干活的是帮手。

**English:**
The shift is from one chat to a fleet of helpers. Two incumbent routes:
workflow-as-code (heavy), or a model keeping the books (untrustworthy).
The third way: a program keeps the books, agents do the work.

## Competitive contrast

**中文：**
LangGraph/Temporal 要你嵌库、写图、养一年，工人是一次看不见的函数调用；
CrewAI/AutoGen 把一群帮手塞进同一个脑子。这里没有库可嵌——文件夹就是流程，
帮手是看得见的窗口，隔离是规矩不是期望。市面上没有一模一样的东西：最像的，
一个靠模型记账，一个是云端黑箱。

**English:**
LangGraph/Temporal: embed a library, own a graph for a year, workers are
invisible calls. CrewAI/AutoGen: a crowd in one brain. Here there is no
library — the folder is the workflow, workers are windows you can open,
isolation is a rule, not a hope. The nearest neighbors keep books with a
model, or run opaque in the cloud.

## Who it's for, and why

**中文：**
想今天就要答案、不想熬一晚调提示词的人；想把循环工程 / 自我改进当实验玩的
人——四个评审并行、各签各的检查单，就是文件夹里四行字，零配置；一个人一台
机器、想交代完就走的人。

**English:**
People who need the answer today, not an evening of prompt tuning; loop
engineers and RSI tinkerers — four parallel reviewers, each signing its own
checklist, is four lines in a folder, zero config; anyone on one machine who
wants to hand off and walk away.

---

## The guardrail — say this, not that

The product's own promise (see `README.md`) is **"run untuned today, tune
from the trace tomorrow"** — *not* "it becomes optimal with zero human
touch." Market it as **"grows toward what actually works"**, never as
"auto-optimal." That is both the honest claim and the differentiation
against every "automatically gets better" pitch out there.
