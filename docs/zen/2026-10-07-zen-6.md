# Decision log — zen 6: born booked, fork, coworker

Verbatim record, 2026-10-07. Born from a failed migration method:
lending a feature across projects via AGENTS.md/ADR docs — stale,
lossy, and the tacit parts never got written down. The fix is live
context, not better docs.

## Zen 6 — does a model gate every birth?

**Tried:** JEV as the only birth canal (zen 1): the model proposes,
policy admits — a one-entry First Mate product that does everything
for you.

**Rejected:** not reusable. The product moved on (the chat becomes a
DAG creator; the entry is `agentic-os run`). The lab path already
births students from the professor's protocol with no model in the
loop, and `src/policy.ts` admits deterministically: slots, retry cap,
one Job. The gate half of JEV was a product idea wearing a rail's
clothes.

**Final:** **Every pane is born booked.** Admission is deterministic
checks; `pane.born` books the decider (`parent`) — and, for a fork,
the `seed`. A model may still propose; its proposal is an input to
booking, never the booking.

**zen.core**
Every pane is born booked. No booked birth, no pane.
The stamp is rail; the proposal is not.

**zen.decision**
The parent stamp stays: zen 4's two wake budgets, the receipt, the
audit, and fail recovery are computed from `pane.born`.
Unbooked live panes remain audit findings.
JEV keeps its name: the check plus the stamp.

## Zen 6 — can a new pane inherit an old conversation?

**Tried:** adopt — enroll a live pane of another session into a new
group, without closing it.

**Rejected:** the pane's record, herdr scope, and binding answer to
another master; the receipt (panes of this Job = 0) goes ambiguous;
the host session may be bound to another worktree — two writers.

**Final:** **Fork.** A birth mode: `seed = {tool, session_id, since}`.
The new pane's host session starts seeded from the old one. Bytes are
never copied; the original is never written; the new pane gets a fresh
record and a fresh binding. A fork passes the same admission, plus the
seed must resolve.

**Why fork beats docs:** a transcript pointer lets any agent read the
old conversation as an archive — it must know what to look for, and
the tacit parts stay tacit. A fork makes the old conversation the new
pane's own context — first-party, live, answerable to questions no doc
anticipated. Docs are write-ahead externalization; fork is inheritance
on demand. Keep both: the pointer is auditability, the fork is
reusability.

**zen.core**
A fork inherits the conversation, not the pane.
The original session is never written.

**zen.decision**
Fork is a birth mode, not an adoption.
`pane.born` books the seed beside the parent.
Adoption of a live pane stays rejected.

## Zen 6 — who owns panes?

**Tried:** "The `shep` ledger owns panes."

**Rejected:** the trace already books every birth and death; shep is
how a split opens, like herdr — a mechanism, not an owner.

**Final:** **The trace owns pane records.** Three owners stand: the
trace (pane records), the Job's dir (progress and output), the
worktree (project bytes). A worker owns none of these.

**zen.core**
Ownership is a fact about who holds the record.
Mechanisms are not owners.

## Zen 6 — may workers depend on each other?

**Tried:** bend fan-out ("workers do not depend on each other") for
pairs that talk.

**Rejected:** that law is fan-out's whole premise.

**Final:** **Coworker** — a third workflow kind. Named members
exchange typed message files on the Job dir; the Rail is the relay; a
worker never writes another worker. The orchestrator pane stays
optional and stays failure-only. Coworker produces a report.
`kind = "coworker"`.

**zen.core**
A worker never writes another worker.
The rail is the relay.

**zen.decision**
What a message is, is a detector (the program's say).
That a message is relayed, not written peer-to-peer, is rail.
Fan-out's independence law is untouched.

## Do not resurrect (zen 6 adds)

- A model's yes as the birth gate
- Adopting a live pane of another session
- A worker writing another worker
- shep or herdr as owners of anything
